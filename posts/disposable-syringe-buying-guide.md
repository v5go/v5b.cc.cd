# Disposable Syringe Specifications: A Complete B2B Buying Guide

> **Author:** V5 Medical Growth Team  
> **Category:** Syringes / B2B Sourcing  
> **Reading Time:** 10 Minutes  
> **Target Audience:** Medical Device Distributors, Hospital Procurement Officers, Global Wholesalers, Tender Bidders

---

## Executive Summary

Disposable syringes are among the highest-volume medical consumables in global healthcare procurement, with billions of units consumed annually across hospitals, clinics, vaccination programs, and laboratories. For B2B buyers, the challenge is navigating a fragmented specification landscape — tip types, barrel designs, volumes, needle configurations, and sterilization standards — while ensuring every lot meets regulatory requirements.

This guide covers the complete specification system, quality red flags, documentation requirements, and procurement strategies for sourcing disposable syringes from China and other manufacturing hubs.

---

## 1. Syringe Anatomy: The 5 Core Components

| Component | Material | Key Spec |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Barrel | Medical-grade polypropylene (PP) | Transparent, clear graduation marks |
| Plunger | PP | Smooth operation, anti-detachment design |
| Gasket/Piston | Latex or latex-free (synthetic rubber) | Latex-free increasingly required in EU/US |
| Needle (if included) | Stainless steel 304 | 16G–30G, EO sterilized |
| Packaging | PE pouch or blister | Individual sterile pack |

## 2. Tip Types: Luer Lock vs Luer Slip vs Catheter Tip

| Tip Type | Connection | Best For |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Luer Lock** | Threaded, screw-on | Secure connection; prevents accidental detachment; standard for most injections |
| **Luer Slip** | Friction-fit, push-on | Quick attach/detach; suitable for low-pressure applications |
| **Catheter Tip** | Tapered, larger bore | Wound irrigation, enteral feeding, non-vascular use |

**Procurement tip:** Luer Lock is the default choice for hospital tenders. Luer Slip is acceptable for high-throughput vaccination programs where speed matters. Always confirm tip compatibility with the needles or catheters in your existing inventory.

## 3. Barrel Design: 2-Part vs 3-Part

| Design | Components | Advantages |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| **2-Part** | Barrel + plunger (no gasket) | Lower cost; suitable for non-critical applications |
| **3-Part** | Barrel + plunger + rubber gasket | Better seal, smoother operation; standard for medical use |

**Market reality:** 3-part syringes dominate hospital procurement. 2-part is mainly for veterinary, industrial, or laboratory use.

## 4. Volume Specifications

| Volume | Common Use |
|--------|-----------|
| 1 ml | Insulin, tuberculin, precise dosing |
| 2 ml / 3 ml | General injections, most common hospital sizes |
| 5 ml | Standard adult injections |
| 10 ml | Larger dose medications, blood draw |
| 20 ml / 30 ml | Irrigation, high-volume injection |
| 50 ml / 60 ml | Enteral feeding, wound irrigation, catheter tip applications |

**Stocking strategy:** 3ml, 5ml, and 10ml cover 70%+ of hospital demand. Start with these three SKUs.

## 5. Needle Configurations

Syringes are supplied **with or without needles**:

- **Without needle (no needle):** For buyers who source needles separately or use needle-free applications
- **With needle:** Fixed needle (insulin syringes) or detachable needle (hypodermic syringes)

| Needle Gauge | Typical Length | Application |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| 18G–21G | 1"–1.5" | Blood draw, viscous medications |
| 22G–23G | 1"–1.25" | Standard IM injections |
| 25G–27G | 5/8"–1" | Subcutaneous, pediatric |

## 6. Sterilization & Packaging

- **Sterilization method:** Ethylene Oxide (EO) gas — industry standard
- **Packaging:** Individual PE pouch or blister pack; 100 pcs/box typical
- **Shelf life:** Usually 3–5 years from sterilization date

**Quality red flags:**
- Blurred or missing lot number / expiration date on individual packs
- Non-medical-grade silicone oil lubricant (must meet ISO 10993 biocompatibility)
- Graduation marks that rub off (test with alcohol wipe)

## 7. Regulatory & Documentation Checklist

For international procurement, require:

- [ ] ISO 13485 quality management system certificate
- [ ] ISO 7886 (sterile hypodermic syringes) compliance
- [ ] CE certificate (EU) or FDA 510(k) (US)
- [ ] EO sterilization validation report
- [ ] Latex-free declaration (if applicable)
- [ ] Free Sale Certificate (for government tenders)

**Quality red flag:** Suppliers who cannot provide ISO 7886 test reports. This is the core product standard — its absence is disqualifying.

## 9. FAQ

**Q: Luer Lock or Luer Slip — which should I stock?**  
A: Luer Lock for hospitals (secure, standard). Luer Slip for high-speed vaccination programs. When in doubt, stock Luer Lock — it is compatible with both Luer Lock and Luer Slip needles.

**Q: 2-part or 3-part?**  
A: 3-part for any medical/human use. 2-part only for veterinary or industrial.

**Q: How do I verify EO sterilization?**  
A: Request the sterilization validation report and check for EO residual testing per ISO 10993-7. Each lot should have a sterilization indicator.

**Q: What is the difference between sterile and non-sterile syringes?**  
A: Medical syringes must be sterile (EO). Non-sterile syringes are for laboratory or industrial use only — never for patient injection.
